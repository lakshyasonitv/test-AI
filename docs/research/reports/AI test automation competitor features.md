# Steal the mechanics, not the marketing

The competitors' real lead over our pipeline is structural, not "agentic". The serious AI test generators look at the screen a step will act on before writing that step. They choose its target from a closed list of elements that actually exist, and they prove the step by running it. ACCELQ, the tool this question centres on, documents mostly a **modelling discipline** rather than AI. Its help centre describes Contexts, Views and Actions, where an Action's Origin must equal the previous step's Destination, a sync element for each Context, and identification by label, anchor and index. Our pipeline already shares the market's single most important idea, deterministic grounding against discovered elements, and is ahead of documented practice in one place: a model proposes and a person approves. Our gaps are elsewhere. On a first attempt the IR is written blind for every screen after the entry page. Discovery follows only links. Element identity is a selector ladder that ranks `#id` above role and label and keeps no persistent fingerprint. Readiness has no network signal that tolerates Salesforce's long-polling. Failures are labelled by how they failed, never by whether the app or the test is at fault. For Salesforce, the most valuable evidence is Salesforce's own. Its 1,638 published UTAM page objects show which DOM hooks Salesforce itself treats as stable, and Copado's open-source QWeb engine shows a readiness wait that excludes CometD long-polls. Most headline "agentic" claims have no documented mechanism behind them. That includes ACCELQ's Analyzer and Discovery agents, Functionize's 99.95% tracking accuracy, Virtuoso's 95% heal accuracy and QA Wolf's "4x". The plan at the end runs in eight phases, numbered 0 to 7, each behind its own flag and testable on its own: an evidence harness, cheap structural correctness fixes, readiness, generation that observes each screen, button-driven discovery, guarded fingerprint healing, a Salesforce component layer, and finally triage, data and reporting.

## Most "agentic" claims rest on marketing, not documentation

The evidence comes from six research streams of very different reliability. Several vendor help centres could not be fetched directly from the research environment: ACCELQ, Provar, Tricentis, mabl, Katalon, Functionize and Testim. What these notes say about those vendors comes from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages. The strongest evidence is code read directly: Copado's QWeb 3.8.3, Salesforce's `salesforce-pageobjects` 12.0.0, Healenium's `tree-comparing` 0.4.14, the Similo and SimiloLLM research code, Playwright's selector generator and documentation sources, Crawljax, browser-use and ReportPortal's documentation repository. The shared search budget ran out before Keysight Eggplant, Copado's closed Salesforce keyword library, Lightning Web Security, synthetic versus native shadow DOM, and Virtuoso's internals could be researched. Treat those as **unknown, not absent**.

ACCELQ shows the divide between documentation and marketing most clearly. Its help centre documents the Context/View/Action model, locator-free identification, context synchronisation and self-healing in detail. Its Release 7.0 notes document the Autopilot features: plain-English logic, "Generate Test Steps", an AI designer for modularity, "Action Insights" and autonomous element healing ([ACCELQ 7.0 release notes](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/33991400145165-What-s-new-in-Release-7-0)). **How a generated step is tied to a real element is not documented anywhere.** The agent lineup also differs between ACCELQ's September 2026 award release and its agentic-automation guide ([ACCELQ award 2026](https://www.accelq.com/news/accelq-named-ai-based-engineering-solution-of-the-year-2026/); [ACCELQ agentic guide](https://www.accelq.com/blog/agentic-automation/)). One independent roundup concludes that most teams use ACCELQ "as a powerful codeless platform with AI assistance rather than agent-driven testing" ([qa.tech](https://qa.tech/blog/best-agentic-qa-tools-in-2026)).

The headline numbers elsewhere are self-reported. They include Functionize's "99.95% accuracy in ML element tracking" ([Functionize](https://www.functionize.com/ml-engine)), Virtuoso's roughly 95% self-heal accuracy ([Virtuoso](https://virtuosoqa.com/solutions/ai-powered-test-automation)), Momentic's "over 99%" cache replay ([Momentic](https://momentic.ai/web-app-testing)) and QA Wolf's claim that its Mapping Agent V3 is "4x more effective" ([QA Wolf](https://www.qawolf.com/mapping-ai)). **No vendor publishes a false-heal rate, a hallucinated-step rate, or the precision of its failure classification.**

What survives scrutiny is a set of mechanisms that recur across independent vendors and match the academic literature. The model chooses its target from a closed set of real elements, and every screen is observed before steps are written for it. Heals apply to the current run, and keeping one needs a person's approval. Deterministic assertions are preferred over AI checks. Failures fall into five buckets, and a failure stays "To Investigate" rather than being assigned a guessed cause. ## The market observes each screen before it writes a step

### Discovery: agents click everything, while research says crawl systematically

Every vendor that explores autonomously runs an LLM agent in a real browser: Octomind, QA Wolf's Mapping Agent, Momentic, Autify Genesis and Rainforest. The agent can act on every interactive element, not only links, which is why button-driven single-page apps are not a special case for them. Login is handled the same way everywhere. Octomind checks first for a cookie banner and a login wall, asks for credentials, generates a login test and adds it "as dependency for other tests" ([Octomind](https://octomind.dev/docs/build-tests/recommended-tests)). QA Wolf's mapper "will ask for your credentials" when it meets a login prompt ([QA Wolf docs](https://docs.qawolf.com/qawolf/Map-your-app-s-workflows-2a45b2a994fb80ca8a87c8fdc2a443fc)). Octomind's own product log shows the limit of this style. The team **moved away from letting the agent freely auto-generate tests** and now offers recommendations for a person to choose from ([Octomind update, July 2025](https://octomind.dev/blog/product-update/25-07-24)). Unsupervised exploration was too noisy to file automatically.

**No vendor documents how its explorer avoids "Delete account" or "Place order"**, and the research is unambiguous that a model must not make that call. On WebGuard's 4,939 human-labelled actions that change application state, frontier models scored **below 60% on predicting action outcomes and below 60% recall on HIGH-risk actions** ([WebGuard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14293)). The safest documented pattern is Meticulous. During replay it answers every request from a recording, so a replayed session cannot change anything in the backend ([Meticulous](https://app.meticulous.ai/docs/concepts/network-recording-and-patching)).

The academic literature points to a deterministic explorer rather than an agent that asks a model about every click. Crawljax remains the baseline. It builds "a state-flow graph of the dynamic DOM states and the event-based transitions between them" ([Crawljax](https://github.com/crawljax/crawljax)) and defaults to clicking each element once, at most two repeats of an explored action, and a one-hour runtime cap ([CrawlRules.java](https://github.com/crawljax/crawljax/blob/master/core/src/main/java/com/crawljax/core/configuration/CrawlRules.java)).

Yandrapally et al. found that "no algorithm is able to accurately detect all functional near-duplicates within apps, without sacrificing coverage" when one whole-page similarity threshold is used ([ICSE 2020](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2020/icse-2020-papers/102/Near-Duplicate-Detection-in-Web-App-Model-Inference)). FragGen compares page fragments instead of whole pages. It detected **123% more near-duplicates**, and its crawl models had 62% more precision and 70% more recall ([FragGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14043v1)). A USENIX Security 2024 study that re-implemented a decade of crawlers found **randomised crawlers beat the commonly used ones** on coverage ([SoK: State of the Krawlers](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/stafeev)). SIBILLA memoises transitions it has observed to behave deterministically, and cut test-generation time by **22–96%** ([SIBILLA, TSE 2024](https://boa.unimib.it/handle/10281/462218)).

LLM-Explorer argues that calling the model on every step is "neither necessary nor effective". It reports higher coverage at more than 148x lower cost by limiting the model to maintaining abstract knowledge about the app ([LLM-Explorer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10593)). The most portable answer to "what is clickable" is browser-use's ordered rule list. Its decisive rule detects JavaScript click listeners through the Chrome DevTools Protocol, which catches React-style `div onClick` controls that cheerio can never see ([browser-use](https://github.com/browser-use/browser-use/blob/main/browser_use/dom/serializer/clickable_elements.py)).

The same literature names two defects in our own same-screen rule. First, `screens.ts` merges a new capture into the **first** stored screen above the 0.7 threshold (`findIndex`), not the closest one. That is the order-dependence Crawljax had to fix by comparing each new state against all known states ([arXiv 1405.0749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0749)). Second, the overlap coefficient `|A∩B|/min(|A|,|B|)` scores 1.0 whenever one label set is a subset of the other. A wizard step that repeats step 1's fields under the same heading and adds more therefore merges into step 1.

### Modelling: ACCELQ's lesson is a typed graph, not AI

ACCELQ models an application as a graph of states and edges. A **Context** is an application state, a **View** is a captured sub-state of a Context, and an **Action** is an edge. Each Action has one Owner Context, one or more Origin Contexts and a Destination Context. The scenario builder **enforces continuity structurally**: "subsequent steps must have an 'Origin Context' that matches the 'Destination Context' of the step just added". Picking an Action out of sequence raises a "Missing Step" alert ([ACCELQ: selecting steps](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360060372771-Selecting-steps-while-building-a-Scenario); [out-of-sequence options](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360060373231-Options-when-selecting-an-out-of-sequence-Action)).

Three refinements make this workable. Validation Actions and modal-type pages get a **"Current Context"** destination, which resolves to wherever the Action was called from ([ACCELQ: destination](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021044992-Defining-Destination-Context-for-an-Action)). An Action such as Login can have **multiple destinations** (the dashboard, password expiry, or back to the login page), and whoever places it in a scenario must pick which one that flow expects ([ACCELQ: multiple destinations](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021308872-Examples-Multiple-Destination-Contexts-for-Action)). Views are kept under discipline: a new View is needed "ONLY if your action logic needs to perform any interaction with the elements that the view will bring", and differences in data never justify new Contexts ([ACCELQ: multiple Views](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000692332-Why-do-I-ever-need-multiple-Views-for-a-Context)).

ACCELQ's modal guide uses the Salesforce "New Lead" modal as its example. It splits opening the modal from working inside it, and shares the inner Action with every Context that can open it ([ACCELQ: modal windows](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001619091-Designing-Contexts-and-Actions-for-modal-windows)). The Application Universe is "formed automatically as part of your test development", meaning it is a by-product of authoring rather than of a crawl ([ACCELQ: Universe](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026201612-Application-Universe)).

The transferable insight is that **continuity is a type check, not a judgement a model makes**. Our persistent screen map already stores screens and click edges, but D-54 limits it to hints for the LLM. Turning those edges into a continuity check and into expected destinations needs no model at all.

Research suggests a stronger use of the same graph. The screen-transition-graph paper uses Dijkstra's shortest path between screens, so the navigation prefix is computed deterministically and the LLM only describes each edge ([STG paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02529v2)). AutoDroid injects memory from earlier exploration into its prompts and reports **90.9% action accuracy, +36.4% over a GPT-4 baseline** ([AutoDroid](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15272)). A June 2026 empirical study adds that LLM testers do best with "concise functionality-level history" rather than raw state dumps ([arXiv 2606.16650](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16650)).

### Generation: closed sets and live verification beat grounding after the fact

The dominant way to author tests is **interleaved act-observe-write**: perform a step in a live browser, observe the result, then write the next step. Playwright's Test Agents divide the work into a Planner that "explores the app and produces a Markdown test plan", a Generator that turns the plan into tests, and a Healer that repairs failing ones ([Playwright test agents](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-agents)). The Generator "performs the scenarios live, verifies selectors and assertions"; that wording reached us through a third-party guide quoting the docs ([guide](https://medium.com/@ismailsobhy/ai-powered-test-automation-part-4-complete-guide-to-playwright-agents-planner-generator-healer-d418166afe34)). Playwright MCP enforces the anti-hallucination rule in its data model. The model can act only on `ref=eN` values from the current accessibility snapshot, and refs "are valid until the page changes, after which a fresh snapshot returns new refs" ([Playwright MCP](https://playwright.dev/mcp/snapshots)).

Other tools follow the same pattern. Reflect's authoring agent can "delete and retry steps when something fails" ([SmartBear](https://smartbear.com/blog/agentic-test-creation-with-reflect)). QA Wolf's Outliner fixes an Arrange-Act-Assert outline before any code is written ([QA Wolf](https://www.qawolf.com/ai)). testRigor has a person approve test-case *names* first, and those names then serve as the prompts for the steps ([testRigor](https://testrigor.com/certification/ai-driven-test-case-generation-with-testrigor/)).

The research agrees on where the errors come from. SeeAct reached **51.1% task success only when its plans were grounded manually** and concluded that "grounding still remains a major challenge" ([SeeAct](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01614)). Mind2Web's MindAct uses a small model to rank every DOM element, then asks the large model a multiple-choice question over the top candidates ([Mind2Web](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06070)). WebTestPilot restricts assertions to operations over abstracted element symbols rather than prose ([WebTestPilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11724v1)). Salva and Taguelmimt show that executing natural-language tests is "inherently unsound". Of eight open models, only the 70B Llama models met their consistency bar, which argues for re-running a new test before blaming the app ([ENASE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19136v2)).

Our pipeline already grounds every IR step against discovered elements, which is the same closed-set idea applied after the fact. The difference is **when the model sees the screen**. On a first attempt our model writes every later step from the request's wording alone (TD-122). Grounding then corrects the names, but it cannot supply a step the model never wrote.

## Identity, waits and healing: semantic first, scored fallback, guarded persistence

### Element identity has converged on what a user sees

ACCELQ's default since release 6.1 is **locator-free** identification, built from three parts. The **label** is visible or related text, a placeholder or button text. The optional **anchor** is text above or to the left of the target, which "limits the search for the label to the area under the designated anchor text". The **index** breaks remaining ties by screen position, counted from the upper-left. Labels and anchors accept equals-ignoring-case, starts-with, ends-with, contains and regex operators ([ACCELQ: locator-free](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/13031514925453-Locator-free-Element-Identification-for-Web-Applications); [ACCELQ: operators](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/19052862960525-Troubleshooting-issues-with-Locator-free-element-identification)). The fallback is a "Smart Locator", an attribute combination chosen by a proprietary algorithm. ACCELQ's Element Explorer shows a **live count of matching elements** as attributes are ticked ([ACCELQ: Element Explorer](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026192152-Setting-up-Element-Identification-with-Element-Explorer)). testRigor formalises the same target-and-anchor idea with words for relative position ([testRigor](https://testrigor.com/blog/how-to-reference-elements-by-ui-position-using-testrigor)). Virtuoso tells duplicates apart with words like "top" and keeps several selectors per element, using the first that resolves ([Virtuoso KB](https://knowledge.virtuoso.qa/hc/en-us/articles/19510251689501-Virtuoso-is-targeting-unexpected-element-type-that-has-the-same-hints)).

Playwright's selector generator gives the clearest published ranking, because its scores are in source code. Lower is better, and the generator never emits `nth` beyond 5 ([selectorGenerator.ts](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/packages/injected/src/selectorGenerator.ts)):

| Locator type | Score |
|---|---|
| Configured test id | 1 |
| Role plus accessible name | 100 |
| Label | 140 |
| Text | 180 |
| CSS `#id` | 500 |
| `nth` | 10000 |

Our discovery ladder puts `#id` above aria-label and role-plus-name, which is the reverse of this order. Playwright's own GUID filter does not protect us either. Run on real framework ids, `isGuidLike` **does not flag** `input-145`, `ember123`, `j_idt42`, `mui-7` or `react-select-3-input`; that was computed in the research notes on the function copied verbatim from the same file. Our Salesforce rule drops `/-\d+$/` ids, but TD-133 shows it also drops meaningful ids such as `sel-102`. The principled fix is evidence across sessions: an id that is identical on two walks is not generated. Sidereal applies the same idea across app versions, learning attribute fragility from earlier releases, and reports **55% fewer broken locators than ROBULA+** ([Sidereal via SEPL](https://sepl.dibris.unige.it/ROBULA.php)). Playwright has also **deprecated** its layout selectors (`:below`, `:right-of`, `:near`), warning that "matching based on layout may produce unexpected results" ([Playwright other locators](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/docs/src/other-locators.md)). Our Salesforce label-anchored rung is built on exactly those selectors.

For a fallback fingerprint, only two systems publish exact weights. Similo compares 14 properties. Tag, name, id, visible text and neighbour text weigh **1.5**, everything else (class, location, area, XPath and so on) weighs **0.5**, and an exact visible-text match doubles its weight ([Similo.java](https://github.com/michelnass/SimiloLLM/blob/main/similollm/Similo.java)). Healenium combines a longest-common-subsequence match of the root-to-target path with a 350-point score per candidate node. Its id term is a fuzzy Levenshtein match, so `input-145` against `input-146` earns about 0.89 of the id points. **That actively rewards a neighbouring auto-generated field**, which is a false-heal path on Lightning ([tree-comparing 0.4.14](https://repo1.maven.org/maven2/com/epam/healenium/tree-comparing/0.4.14/tree-comparing-0.4.14-sources.jar)).

Similo's replication data puts numbers on the trade-off. Across 48 apps and 801 targets, Similo located **710** correctly against **587** for a multi-locator baseline. On the research notes' reading of the result columns, **all 91 of Similo's errors were silent wrong-element picks**, while the multi-locator baseline made none ([Similo2 results](https://github.com/michelnass/Similo2/blob/main/WidgetLocator/FinalResultsTable.txt)). Scoring buys recall, but it needs a separate guard against confident wrong answers.

### Waiting: actionability per action, readiness per screen

Playwright's model is the reference. Each action waits for its own checks: the element is visible, stable for two animation frames, receives the pointer event, and is enabled and editable ([Playwright actionability](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/docs/src/actionability.md)). `networkidle` is marked **"DISCOURAGED … rely on web assertions to assess readiness instead"** ([Playwright params](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/docs/src/api/params.md)). A click on a page that is still hydrating "won't have any effect" ([Playwright navigations](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/docs/src/navigations.md)). Katalon's opaque Smart Wait shows the cost of a blanket wait for the DOM to go quiet. One user's suite went from **3m 24s to 7m 30s** after an upgrade ([Katalon forum](https://forum.katalon.com/t/katalon-10-3-1-smart-wait-is-now-much-much-slower/180887)).

ACCELQ adds a readiness rule for each state. A Context's sync element must be **unique to that page, stable, and late-loading**, because an element shared with other pages "can falsely confirm" that the page has loaded ([ACCELQ sync guidelines](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005242267-Guidelines-for-setting-up-Synchcronization)). ACCELQ also separates *inspection* commands, which return immediately, from *verify* commands, which wait up to the element timeout ([ACCELQ wait times](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030131851-Wait-times-for-various-commands-and-UI-interactions-during-test-execution)).

The most concrete readiness algorithm comes from Copado's open-source QWeb. Its default wait first requires `document.readyState` to be complete. It then waits until no fetch or XHR requests are pending, counted by an injected monitor that **ignores any XHR whose body contains `"connectionType":"long-polling"`**. That body marks Salesforce's CometD streaming connection, which would otherwise keep "network idle" false forever. Next it waits until no spinner is busy, and a spinner counts only if it **actually paints and is topmost** at three sample points. Last, it waits for the DOM to stay quiet for 200 ms, capped at 1.5 s. On timeout it continues rather than failing ([QWeb xhr.py](https://github.com/qentinelqi/qweb/blob/master/QWeb/internal/xhr.py); [install_monitor.js](https://github.com/qentinelqi/qweb/blob/master/QWeb/internal/js/install_monitor.js); [spinner_busy.js](https://github.com/qentinelqi/qweb/blob/master/QWeb/internal/js/spinner_busy.js)). Momentic waits with a 3 s default ([Momentic smart waiting](https://docs.momentic.ai/essentials/smart-waiting)), and its list of wait signals includes **same-origin requests** only, so analytics beacons do not block it ([Momentic auto-heal](https://momentic.ai/docs/auto-heal)).

Since TD-121, our screen capture waits for the page's own fetch/XHR traffic to drain, but that wait has neither a long-poll exclusion nor a same-origin filter. We infer that on a Lightning org with streaming enabled, every capture will wait out its full bound; this should be confirmed against saved run timings. Separately, the generator still emits a fixed `waitForTimeout(500)` after opening a dropdown. That is exactly the kind of sleep Playwright, Cypress and ACCELQ's "Action Insights" steer users away from.

### Healing: the industry separates "heal this run" from "keep the heal"

Every commercial tool heals at runtime so the run can continue, but they differ on what happens next. Katalon saves the healed locator "temporarily" until a person approves it, and shows the proposal with a screenshot ([Katalon](https://docs.katalon.com/katalon-studio/maintain-tests/self-healing-tests-in-katalon-studio)). Healenium logs heals to a database, and a developer applies them in the IDE ([Healenium](https://github.com/healenium/healenium-web)). Octomind "never pushes code to your repository automatically" ([Octomind](https://octomind.dev/product/playwright-self-healing/)). mabl keeps heals per environment with rollback, but warns that **rolling back does not stop the same heal from recurring** ([mabl](https://help.mabl.com/docs/how-auto-heal-works)). A rejected heal therefore has to be stored as a negative example, not merely reverted.

The documented guards against false heals fall into six kinds. Healenium sets a **score floor** with `score-cap`, whose example value is 0.5. It also accepts a healed locator only when it finds **exactly one match that no other step already uses** ([Healenium HealingService](https://github.com/healenium/healenium-web/blob/master/src/main/java/com/epam/healenium/service/HealingService.java)). mabl and Testim **never heal a locator a person wrote or customised**. Healenium's `@DisableHealing` and ACCELQ **never heal absence checks**; ACCELQ skips healing "where the element's presence is doubtful" ([ACCELQ self-healing](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420289421709-Self-Healing-Comprehensive-approach-to-adaptive-element-identification)). Checksum **keeps assertions unchanged** while it repairs actions ([Checksum](https://npmjs.com/package/@checksum-ai/runtime)). Finally, the tools **refuse to heal a real regression**. Playwright's Healer outputs "a skipped test if the healer believes that functionality is broken" ([Playwright agents](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/main/docs/src/test-agents-js.md)), and Momentic tries failure recovery only on tests that passed at least once in the last seven days ([Momentic](https://momentic.ai/docs/platform/auto-healing/failure-recovery)).

The academic pattern for using an LLM safely in healing is VON Similo LLM. A deterministic scorer shortlists the top 10 candidates, and the model may answer only with "the widget_id number (digits) only". The experiment code also computes an outlier test, `Q3 + 1.5×IQR` over the top-10 scores, which can mark when the scorer's best candidate stands out clearly enough to trust without the model ([SimiloLLMTest.java](https://github.com/michelnass/SimiloLLM/blob/main/similollm/SimiloLLMTest.java)).

Our D-27 rule ("a model proposes, it never writes") already matches the stricter half of the market. What is missing is the fingerprint, the score floor, the margin test and a memory of rejected heals. One caveat on priority: healing only pays when saved tests are re-run. For one-shot generate-and-run use, better generation and discovery buy more than better healing.

## Verification, data, triage and reporting: deterministic checks and honest buckets

### Verification: AI checks are the fallible tier, and nobody checks for vacuity

The vendors that sell AI assertions document their limits. mabl warns that a vague GenAI assertion such as "the page has loaded" **"could pass even if a submit button is missing"**, and advises strict prompts that test one thing each ([mabl GenAI guidance](https://help.mabl.com/hc/en-us/articles/28810650854292)). Momentic never caches "AI check" steps ([Momentic step cache](https://momentic.ai/docs/reliability/step-cache)), and recommends its exact-text check "because it is deterministic and cheaper" ([Momentic](https://momentic.ai/docs/reference/mobile-commands/assert.md)). testRigor warns that its AI validations "can occasionally produce incorrect results (hallucinations)" ([testRigor](https://testrigor.com/certification/validations/)). Applitools advertises "deterministic test execution (no LLMs at runtime)" ([Applitools](https://applitools.com/platform-overview/)), and mabl reports visual changes as **warnings, not failures** ([mabl visual change](https://api.help.mabl.com/docs/visual-change-detection)).

On the deterministic side, Applitools defaults to a Strict match with Layout regions over dynamic content, and its root-cause view diffs the stored DOM and CSS behind a visual change ([Applitools match levels](https://applitools.com/docs/eyes/concepts/best-practices/match-levels); [Applitools RCA](https://applitools.com/docs/eyes/concepts/reviewing-tests/root-cause-analysis)). mabl's API steps assert status 200 by default ([mabl API steps](https://help.mabl.com/docs/api-steps)). Playwright's auto-retrying assertions include ARIA snapshots with partial matching and regex names, and `expect(response).toBeOK()` ([Playwright assertions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/playwright/main/docs/src/test-assertions-js.md); [ARIA snapshots](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/playwright/main/docs/src/aria-snapshots.md)).

**No vendor documents an automatic vacuity check**: a rule that an assertion must be false before the action that should cause it and true after. mabl's only mitigation is guidance on how to write assertions. Our own records show this exact class of bug: `url_contains "#"` (TD-120), and a stalled form that passed by asserting the validation error it was stuck on (TD-131). The fix for TD-131 is still limited to Salesforce runs with quoted expectations. A structural vacuity check would go beyond documented practice, which also means no vendor evidence exists on how well it works, so it must be proven by replaying saved runs.

### Test data: declared value lists, leases and API seeding, never invented values

The tools that document combinatorial test generation do it as pure algorithm. ACCELQ runs "all-pair analysis" to find the smallest set of combinations that covers each value of every parameter bound to a Data Type or Data List, then lets the user pick from the proposal ([ACCELQ](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034374472-Test-Case-Generation-for-Manual-Testing)). Tosca offers all combinations, orthogonal, pairwise and **linear expansion**, which changes one attribute at a time so that a failure points to a single field. Its default cap is 1,000 combinations ([Tosca combinatorics](https://docs.tricentis.com/tosca-2026.1/en-us/content/testcase_design/instance_combinatorics.htm)). Tosca's Test Data Service marks a record as consumed while a run uses it and releases it afterwards, with a read-only mode for data that many tests share ([Tosca TDS](https://documentation.tricentis.com/tosca/1320/en/content/test_data_management/tds_expert_module.htm)). Applitools' docs say it generates LLM data "in advance" so runs stay predictable, although one of its blog posts says "at runtime" ([Applitools data generation](https://applitools.com/docs/autonomous/content/topics/llm-assisted_test_data_generation.htm)). Momentic provisions throwaway email inboxes and tells users to "always pass afterDate to avoid matching a stale code from a previous run" ([Momentic OTP](https://momentic.ai/docs/guides/auth/email-otp)).

**No vendor documents deriving valid values from the constraints declared in the DOM.** The browser already exposes those constraints deterministically through `ValidityState`, which reports `patternMismatch`, `rangeOverflow`, `tooLong`, `typeMismatch` and the rest ([MDN ValidityState](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/content/main/files/en-us/web/api/validitystate/index.md)). FormNexus has an LLM propose field constraints and revise them from the responses to real submissions. It reached **89% coverage of form-submission states, 25 points above the best baseline** ([FormNexus, ISSTA 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00950v2)). A study of 11 LLMs on 146 forms found that **complete, clean form context** in the prompt produced the best results ([web-form study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.09965)). For phone numbers, libphonenumber returns valid example numbers per region, and invalid ones for negative tests ([libphonenumber](https://libphonenumber-for-php.readthedocs.io/en/latest/PhoneNumberUtil/)).

### Triage: five buckets, human labels win, and "To Investigate" is the honest default

The market has converged on a business-level taxonomy with five buckets: **Product Bug, Automation Bug, System/Environment Issue, No Defect and To Investigate**. Each tool assigns a bucket by similarity to failures a person has already labelled.

ReportPortal documents its method in full. It indexes only ERROR-level logs, extracts messages, stack traces, numbers, URLs and paths into separate fields, and retrieves the 10 most similar past items by TF-IDF. An XGBoost model then picks the defect type, which is applied **only when its probability is 50% or higher** ([ReportPortal auto-analysis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/reportportal/docs/develop/docs/analysis/AutoAnalysisOfLaunches.mdx)). Its regex-based Pattern Analysis runs *before* the ML ([ReportPortal patterns](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/reportportal/docs/develop/docs/analysis/PatternAnalysis.mdx)). Grouping by unique error means that "with 20 failures, you might see 8 errors" ([ReportPortal handbook](https://reportportal.io/blog/reportportal-handbook/)).

BrowserStack's automatic analysis runs only when a team has already tagged some failures by hand ([BrowserStack](https://www.browserstack.com/docs/test-reporting-and-analytics/features/auto-failure-analysis)). Its default tags mark a test as Flaky when it passed on a retry or flipped status more than 50% of the time over the last 10 runs. A test is a New Failure when it fails with a new error for the first time in 5 runs, and Always Failing when the same error repeats across the last 5 ([BrowserStack Smart Tags](https://www.browserstack.com/docs/test-reporting-and-analytics/features/smart-tags)). mabl picks either a built-in reason or a reason the team defined, and **manual assignments are never overwritten** ([mabl](https://help.mabl.com/hc/en-us/articles/50902973104916-2026-07-10-Automatic-failure-categorization)). Functionize designs its root-cause analysis around the observation that "the root cause may have occurred several steps earlier" ([Functionize](https://www.functionize.com/blog/functionize-root-cause-analysis-self-heal)).

ACCELQ documents one-click defect creation, pre-filled with steps to reproduce and screenshots ([ACCELQ](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/115010371208-How-to-create-Bugs-from-the-Test-result)). Its "AI root cause analysis" appears only on a marketing page ([ACCELQ blog](https://www.accelq.com/blog/bug-tracking-tools/)), and G2 reviewers complain of "limited reporting depth" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/accelq/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons)).

Our `classify.ts` categories, such as `timeout`, `element_missing` and `assertion_failed`, describe **mechanisms, not verdicts**. A timeout can belong to any of the five buckets. The layer we are missing is the business-level bucket on top, defaulting to To Investigate.

### Reporting: the trace we already keep is never shown

Playwright's trace viewer already gives per-action Before, Action and After DOM snapshots, network and console logs scoped to the selected action, and an error marker on the timeline ([Playwright trace viewer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/playwright/main/docs/src/trace-viewer.md)). Our `playwright.config.ts` already sets `trace: "retain-on-failure"`, but the HTML report never links the trace.

One precondition comes before linking it. `scrubServedSecrets` in `executor.ts` rewrites `results.json`, `final-page.txt` and the error-context files. **It does not touch `trace.zip`**, which is written to the same served `artifacts` directory. The generated spec fills credentials from `process.env`, so a failed login trace probably records the real value in its action log. This comes from reading the code and has not yet been confirmed with a canary value. It belongs in `TECH_DEBT.md`, which owns defects.

Two cheap additions round out the report: comparison with **the last passing run of the same steps** ([Perfecto](https://help.perfecto.io/perfecto-help/content/perfecto/test-analysis/str-identify-test-failure-cause.htm)), and ACCELQ's **"Optimal" screenshot policy**, which captures at transition points rather than at every step ([ACCELQ](https://support.accelq.com/hc/en-us/articles/115010299188-Running-Automation-Test)).

## Salesforce publishes its own stable hooks, so mine them as data

Salesforce says openly that "with Lightning, Salesforce made a conscious decision to hide identifiers on its elements", and that shadow boundaries "break traditional means of locating elements" ([Salesforce Developers blog](https://developer.salesforce.com/blogs/2020/01/ui-test-automation-on-salesforce)). Its `aura:waiting` event is deprecated ([Salesforce](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/lightning-component-reference/guide/aura-waiting.html)), so there is no supported hook that says Lightning is idle. Readiness has to be inferred from what can be observed on the page.

Salesforce's sanctioned answer to locating elements is UTAM, a set of JSON page objects that model shadow boundaries explicitly ([UTAM](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/utam/guide/what-is-utam.html)). The published library is versioned **once per Salesforce release**. The Spring '26 package, version 12.0.0, published on 9 March 2026, contains **1,638 page objects, 1,061 of which declare a shadow block**. No Summer '26 or Winter '27 version had appeared by 15 October 2026 ([salesforce-pageobjects](https://www.npmjs.com/package/salesforce-pageobjects)). The official runtime is WebdriverIO. The official recipes warn that "if your org has customizations, you might need to modify page objects or tests locally" ([utam-js-recipes](https://github.com/salesforce/utam-js-recipes)). Playwright support exists only through a community adapter with a single maintainer, created in July 2026 ([playwright-utam](https://www.npmjs.com/package/playwright-utam)).

For an LLM pipeline, the value of these page objects lies in **their data, not their runtime**. Counting attribute selectors across all 1,638 objects shows what Salesforce relies on. Generated ids are absent; most `id` uses are partial matches.

| Hook | Uses | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| `slot` | 149 | edit vs view state (`[slot='inputField']`, `[slot='outputField']`) |
| `data-id` | 113 | component-author ids |
| `role` | 60 | ARIA roles, e.g. `input[role='combobox']` |
| `data-field` | 13 | compound name and address sub-fields |
| `data-target-selection-name` | 7 | "the target field's API name" on record layout items |

The same objects encode an interaction recipe for each component ([salesforce-pageobjects](https://www.npmjs.com/package/salesforce-pageobjects)). A **combobox** is used by clicking its trigger, waiting for `div.slds-dropdown_fluid`, picking `lightning-base-combobox-item[data-value='…']` (the picklist's API value) and then verifying the trigger's `data-value`. A **picklist** can render as any of `lightning-combobox`, `lightning-dual-listbox` or `lightning-select`. A **lookup** is typed into, then its suggestions are awaited, then the exact label is clicked while skipping the `actionAdvancedSearch` and `actionCreateNew` items, and finally the selection pill is checked. A **text input** is cleared with select-all and Delete, its value is **read back**, and `clear()` is used if any text remains. A **record page** is ready once `one-record-home-flexipage2` contains `.forcegenerated-flexipage-module`. Classic and Setup pages run inside `force-aloha-page › iframe[name^=vfFrameId]`.

The package ships under Salesforce's own Terms of Use rather than MIT, including indemnity and usage-data clauses. Re-deriving these facts into our own table is lower-risk than copying the files into the repo.

The commercial specialists take three different routes. **Provar** binds steps to org **metadata**, meaning object and field API names pulled from a connected org into a refreshable cache, and its own runtime maps that metadata to the DOM with each release ([Provar metadata](https://provar.com/blog/product/provar-automations-most-relevant-features-salesforce-metadata-aware-authoring/); [Provar org cache](https://documentation.provar.com/documentation/reload-org-cache/)). It suggests a "By SLDS Label" locator for Lightning fields ([Provar Lightning](https://documentation.provar.com/documentation/salesforce-testing/salesforce-lightning-testing/)). As of Spring '25, its NitroX component mapping was **not on by default for Experience Cloud** ([Provar release notes](https://documentation.provar.com/?p=35523)).

**Copado Robotic Testing** targets visible text with keywords such as `ClickText` and `TypeText` ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/copado-robotic-testing-for-salesforce/automate-tests-with-copado-robotic-testing)). Its QWeb engine restricts search to an open modal (`IsModalXpath`), the same idea as our dialog-first scoping (`DIALOG_SELECTOR` in `targetResolver.ts`). It keeps shadow-DOM search off by default. It also retargets text that Salesforce places directly in a `<slot>` to the clickable parent ([QWeb config](https://github.com/qentinelqi/qweb/blob/master/QWeb/keywords/config.py); [QWeb text.py](https://github.com/qentinelqi/qweb/blob/master/QWeb/internal/text.py)).

**Tosca's** Salesforce Scan builds a module for every control. Its own docs concede that **customised layouts produce non-unique modules** and point users to generic scanning instead ([Tosca](https://documentation.tricentis.com/tosca/1600/en/content/engines_3.0/salesforce/sfdc_sitescanner.htm)). The lesson is that scoping to a container only works when the container's name is itself stable.

Testim's Salesforce claims are marketing ([Testim](https://testim.io/salesforce)). Its documentation does give one concrete convention: name records with a fixed prefix plus a random suffix, and sweep them with Apex, deleting children before parents ([Testim docs](https://docs.tricentis.com/testim/content/salesforce-testing/best-practice-variable-naming-convention-for-easy-cleanup.htm)). ACCELQ documents almost nothing Salesforce-specific beyond its modal guide and its API setup. Its "Salesforce Live" assets that "automatically update test assets with every new vendor release" are marketing ([ACCELQ Salesforce Live](https://www.accelq.com/blog/salesforce-live-the-roi-booster/)).

On test data, every specialist creates prerequisite records **through the API, not the UI**, captures their ids and deletes them afterwards ([Provar teardown](https://documentation.provar.com/documentation/support/setup-and-teardown-test-cases)). Salesforce added an Apex `@TearDown` annotation as a developer preview in Summer '26 ([Salesforce TearDown](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.apexcode.meta/api/apex_classes_annotation_TearDown.htm)).

**Experience Cloud is weak everywhere.** UTAM ships only a handful of `dxp_*` and `community_layout` page objects, and Provar's support is opt-in. On `my.site.com` sites, the generic layers of this plan are what will carry the work: role and accessible name, the readiness probe, act-observe-write, and fingerprint healing.

Four Salesforce facts remain unverified: the exact value format of `data-target-selection-name` on record fields; whether `frontdoor.jsp?sid=` still accepts a session id in 2026 orgs; how synthetic versus native shadow DOM affects our walker of open shadow roots; and what Lightning Web Security changes for external automation. One of our own inferences also matters for the plan. To our knowledge, Lightning's Aura framework sends its server actions, reads included, as POST requests. If so, a discovery safety rule that blocks every non-GET request would break Salesforce discovery, so it has to be checked on a sandbox org before it is enabled there.

## An eight-phase plan, each phase shippable behind its own flag

Every item below follows the repo's platform rules. Each ships behind an env flag that defaults to OFF, so that with every flag off the tool behaves exactly as it does today (rules 2 and 7). Each new check reads IR or AppModel structure, never model-written prose (the central design rule and TD-01). New UI capability gets a new route (rule 1), and no item adds a model call to the generator or executor (D-06). Any `page.evaluate` callback or init script is fully inlined (TD-40), and cache keys gain every new input dimension (TD-22).

**Effort** is a relative estimate that includes tests and the phase report: **S** is up to about three engineer-days, **M** about one to two weeks, and **L** three weeks or more. **Priority** P1 means do it first within its phase. **Verification** climbs one ladder throughout: first a real-Chromium unit test on synthetic HTML, which also satisfies D-19 for any emitted Playwright expression; then artifact replay over saved `runs/`; then at most one paid live confirmation, announced before the money is spent.

The phases are ordered by dependency and then by value. Phase 0 calibrates the thresholds that Phases 4 and 5 rely on, and Phase 2 makes screen captures trustworthy before Phase 3 builds on them. Phase 7 touches only `classify.ts`, the report and data generation, so it can run in parallel with Phases 3 to 6.

If only five things are done, do them in this order: the vacuity check (1.1), first-attempt screen context (3.2), the network-aware settle (2.1), volatile-id detection with the ladder re-rank (1.5), and best-match merging with the button-driven explorer (4.1 and 4.4).

### Phase 0 — Build the evidence harness first

Yandrapally et al. showed that state-equivalence thresholds depend on the app, and every number in Phases 4 and 5 is a starting guess. This phase turns `runs/` into labelled data sets so that later changes can be scored offline at no model cost. It also closes a possible credential exposure before any trace is linked. The phase is done when the harness reports the current rules' precision and recall reproducibly, and no served artifact contains a canary secret.

| 0.1 | Thresholds (0.7 overlap, heal floor, top-k) are guesses | Build labelled data sets from `runs/`: screen pairs (clone, near-duplicate, distinct), element pairs across two sessions, and failed cases with a human defect label ([Yandrapally](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2020/icse-2020-papers/102/Near-Duplicate-Detection-in-Web-App-Model-Inference); [Similo2 oracle data](https://github.com/michelnass/Similo2/blob/main/WidgetLocator/FinalResultsTable.txt)) | Both | M | P1 | Two harness runs give identical numbers; the fixtures contain no secrets |

## Conclusion

The research changes where effort should go. The market's best tools are not better at writing steps. They write them **later**: after the screen exists, against a list of elements the model cannot step outside, and only keeping each step and assertion after it has run. Our pipeline already has the verification half of that design: deterministic grounding, human approval and screen capture. Moving the LLM call inside the replay loop, and handing the model a closed set of real elements, is worth more than any new healing or vision feature. It is also cheaper than it sounds, because batching per screen and computing navigation prefixes from the graph keep the number of model calls close to today's.

Two less obvious conclusions follow. First, the most valuable Salesforce knowledge is free but perishable. Salesforce publishes its own stable hooks each release as a versioned data package, so they belong in a mined table with the release tag in every cache key, not in hand-written prompt rules. Experience Cloud gets almost nothing from any vendor, so there the generic layers are the Salesforce strategy too. Second, three of this plan's strongest guards have **no vendor precedent to borrow evidence from**: the vacuity check, network-level safety during exploration, and detecting volatile ids by comparing sessions. That makes the Phase 0 harness a requirement, not a nicety. The literature's clearest lesson, from Yandrapally's near-duplicates to Similo's silent wrong picks, is that every threshold here depends on the app, and an unmeasured rule is only a guess.
